Twenty-four tracks.
One very busy year.

In 2005 the Atlantic produced so many storms that forecasters ran out of names and, for the first time, switched to the Greek alphabet. This map follows 24 of them, coloured by how intense they got.

Try it: click a track for its details, hover to highlight it, switch the basemap or hide intensity classes (top right), measure a distance with the ruler (top left), and use the globe and crosshair buttons to jump between the whole Atlantic and the Gulf of Mexico.

How to read it

  • Colour is peak intensity on the Saffir–Simpson scale, worked out from each storm’s maximum sustained wind (MaxWind, in knots). Lines also get thicker as the wind gets stronger.
  • The white-rimmed dot marks the first recorded position of each track.
  • The white ring in Louisiana marks where Katrina came ashore. Its track in the data passes within about six kilometres of that spot.

Things to look for

  • The Gulf and the Caribbean are the hot zone. The four Category 5 storms (Emily, Katrina, Rita and Wilma) all cut through the Caribbean or the Gulf of Mexico.
  • The alphabet ran out. Alpha, Gamma, Delta and Epsilon are the names storms got after the 21 on the official list had been used up.
  • Some tracks keep going. The northernmost point reaches 68.8°N, far beyond the tropics.
